What If I Got Made Redundant — Would I Be Okay?

Part of the FirePath Learning Lab · Educational only, not financial advice · Browse all topics →

One number answers this entire question: how many months could you cover your costs with zero income? Most people have never calculated it. This is a useful thing to work out — not to induce anxiety, but because knowing the real number tends to be more reassuring than not knowing.

The concept that answers this: your runway

Your financial "runway" is simply your accessible savings divided by your monthly essential expenses — the number of months you could cover your costs with no income at all. This is the single most useful number for genuinely answering "would I be okay."

Common misconception

"I'd be fine, I'll just find another job quickly." Maybe — but "would I be okay" is really asking what happens if that takes longer than expected. Your runway number answers that directly, rather than hoping for the best case.

A quick example

Josh has $18,000 in accessible savings and $4,500 in essential monthly expenses — a runway of exactly four months. Knowing that specific number, rather than a vague sense of "I'd probably manage," let him decide deliberately whether four months felt like enough buffer for his industry, or whether it was worth building toward six.

A common (though personal) target

Some financial educators suggest three to six months of essential expenses held in an accessible emergency fund — though the right amount depends on your job security, industry, and household situation.

What redundancy payouts add to the picture

In many cases, redundancy comes with a statutory or negotiated payout based on length of service — worth factoring into your runway calculation if applicable to your employment situation, though eligibility and amounts vary and are worth checking against your specific award or contract.

If your runway feels short, that's useful information, not proof that everything is precarious. If it's solid, that's reassuring, evidence-based confidence rather than a hopeful guess.
