Is Renting Actually Cheaper Than Owning Long-Term?

Part of the FirePath Learning Lab · Educational only, not financial advice · Browse all topics →

This question gets strong opinions and weak maths in equal measure online. A genuine comparison requires looking past the monthly repayment-versus-rent headline and into the full picture.

What the simple comparison misses

Comparing a monthly mortgage repayment to monthly rent ignores several real costs and benefits on both sides: the opportunity cost of a deposit that could have been invested instead, ongoing maintenance and rates for owners, equity building through principal repayments, and the fact that rent tends to rise over time while a fixed-rate mortgage repayment (on the same loan) doesn't.

Common misconception

"Rent is always dead money, a mortgage always builds wealth." A mortgage builds wealth through equity, yes — but it also comes with interest, maintenance, and rates that are just as "gone" as rent is. The comparison is closer than the slogan suggests.

A quick example

In a market where a $700,000 property rents for $550 a week but would cost $850 a week to service via mortgage plus rates and maintenance, the renter investing that $300 weekly difference (plus the deposit that would have gone toward the purchase) can build a genuinely competitive alternative portfolio — depending on how the property and share market perform over the specific years compared.

The fairest comparison

The fairest comparison models total cost of ownership (deposit opportunity cost + mortgage interest + rates + maintenance, minus equity built) against total cost of renting (rent paid over time, with the deposit instead invested) — over the same specific time horizon.

Why the answer varies so much by market

In markets with high property prices relative to rent, the numbers can favour renting-and-investing for a period. In markets with lower purchase prices relative to rent, buying can come out ahead faster. There is no single Australia-wide answer — it depends heavily on the specific city, property type, and time period being compared. For the broader trade-off, Should I Keep Renting or Buy a House? covers the FIRE-specific framing.

Neither renting nor owning is universally cheaper long-term — it's a calculation worth doing with your actual local numbers, not a slogan to pick a side on.
