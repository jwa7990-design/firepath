Can I Retire Early If I Bought a House Late?

Part of the FirePath Learning Lab · Educational only, not financial advice · Browse all topics →

A late entry into home ownership can feel like it's closed the door on early retirement — but the actual impact is usually smaller and more specific than it feels.

What buying a house late actually changes

The main effect is a delayed start on redirecting a full income toward investing, since a chunk of it went into a deposit and now goes toward mortgage repayments instead. It's a real cost — but it's not the same as having no assets. Equity in the home is still wealth, even if it's less liquid than shares.

Common misconception

"Buying a house at 45 means I've lost my chance at FIRE." A mortgage delays the timeline — it doesn't erase it. Equity is still real wealth, just structured differently to a share portfolio.

A quick example

Peter bought his first home at 44 with a 25-year mortgage. He'd assumed this ended any chance of early retirement. Once he modelled it properly — treating his growing equity as part of net worth, while continuing modest investing outside the mortgage — his projected freedom date moved from a hoped-for 58 to a realistic 63. Later than the forums suggested, but far from impossible.

Modelling the real timeline

The calculation is the same as always — spending, savings rate, current position — but "current position" now includes home equity as part of net worth, even if it's treated separately from the liquid investments funding day-to-day retirement income. Some people choose to factor in eventually downsizing as part of their plan; others plan around their non-property investments alone and treat the home as separate. If downsizing is part of your thinking, What If I Sold My House — Could I Retire? models exactly that scenario.

The honest bottom line

A late house purchase shifts the timeline later, in proportion to how much of your income and savings it absorbed — it doesn't usually make early retirement mathematically impossible, just a later or more gradual version of the same plan.

Running your actual numbers, rather than assuming the door is closed, is the only way to know your real answer.
