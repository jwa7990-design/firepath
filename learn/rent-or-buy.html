Should I Keep Renting or Buy a House?

Part of the FirePath Learning Lab · Educational only, not financial advice · Browse all topics →

"Renting is throwing money away." You've heard this a hundred times, usually from someone who's never actually run the numbers. Most rent-vs-buy articles focus on lifestyle and stability — from a financial independence angle, the more useful question is how each path affects your freedom timeline.

The renting-and-investing path

Renting keeps a deposit and ongoing savings free to invest in shares or other assets instead of tied up in property equity. This path trades housing stability and forced equity-building for flexibility and, potentially, different growth — depending on how the invested alternative performs against property in your specific market and timeframe.

Common misconception

"Renting is always throwing money away compared to buying." Not necessarily — it depends entirely on what the alternative deposit and repayment difference would have earned if invested instead. Sometimes renting-and-investing wins; sometimes buying does. It's market and timeframe dependent.

A quick example

Two colleagues, similar income. One buys, putting a $100,000 deposit into a home and directing extra income to the mortgage. The other rents a similar property and invests the equivalent $100,000 plus the repayment-vs-rent difference into shares. Over ten years, which one ends up further ahead depends heavily on local property growth versus share market growth over that exact window — there's no universal winner baked into the comparison.

The buying-and-building-equity path

Buying converts repayments partly into forced equity-building rather than pure investment contributions, and removes exposure to rent increases over time — but it also concentrates a large share of wealth into a single asset and location, and reduces liquidity. See How Does Buying a House Delay Financial Independence? for the mechanics of that trade-off.

What deserves the real comparison

A genuinely useful comparison models the total cost of renting plus investing the difference, against the total cost of buying (deposit, mortgage, rates, maintenance) plus the equity built — over your specific likely timeframe, in your specific market. Generic online debates rarely capture this level of personal detail.

This is a circumstance-dependent decision, not one with a single right answer for everyone chasing financial independence.
