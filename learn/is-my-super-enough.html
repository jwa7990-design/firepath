Is My Super Enough to Retire On?

Part of the FirePath Learning Lab · Educational only, not financial advice · Browse all topics →

"Super is basically retirement sorted." Or: "Super definitely won't be enough, you need to do more." Both get repeated constantly, and both are guesses dressed up as certainty. For many Australians, super alone isn't quite enough for the retirement lifestyle they picture — which is exactly why it's worth checking properly rather than assuming either way.

Super as one of two timelines

A useful way to think about this: most people are building wealth on two separate timelines at once — savings and investments outside super (accessible any time), and super itself (generally locked until preservation age, currently 60 for most people). Checking whether "super is enough" really means checking whether the super timeline alone covers your target retirement spending from the age it becomes accessible onward.

Common misconception

"Super is designed to be enough on its own." For many people it covers a meaningful share of retirement needs but not all of it — particularly for anyone planning to stop working before preservation age, or aiming for a higher spending lifestyle than the system's default assumptions.

A quick example

Projected forward, David's super reaches roughly $780,000 by 60 — comfortably above the 25x-spending target for his planned $28,000-a-year "super-funded" portion of retirement. But he wants $60,000 a year overall, meaning $32,000 a year still needs to come from savings outside super. Super alone covers part of the picture clearly — not the whole thing.

How to check

Project your current super balance and contributions forward to your target retirement age, and compare the result to 25 times your planned annual spending from that age onward. If the projected balance covers it, super alone may be sufficient for that phase of retirement — many people still choose to build savings outside super as well, for accessibility before that age and general flexibility. If you're planning to stop working before 60, What Happens to Your Money Before You Can Access Super? covers the gap that creates.

Super is a powerful, tax-advantaged piece of the puzzle — but "enough" depends entirely on your specific spending target, not a generic industry benchmark.
