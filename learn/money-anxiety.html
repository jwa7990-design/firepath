How Do I Stop Feeling Anxious About Money?

Part of the FirePath Learning Lab · Educational only, not financial advice · Browse all topics →

Money anxiety usually isn't really about the number — it's about not knowing the number. Uncertainty is a heavier weight to carry than most actual financial realities turn out to be.

Why not knowing feels worse than knowing

An unclear financial picture leaves room for the mind to imagine the worst version of reality. Once the actual numbers are on the table — savings, spending, debt, trajectory — the picture is almost always more manageable than the vague dread was, even when there's genuine work to do.

Common misconception

"Looking at my real numbers will make the anxiety worse." For most people it's the opposite. A specific, known problem is almost always less frightening to sit with than an unknown one.

A quick example

Renee avoided checking her super and savings balances for two years, dreading what she'd find. When she finally looked, her position was stronger than she'd feared — not perfect, but workable, with a clear next step (increase her savings rate by 5%). The anxiety had outpaced the reality by a wide margin.

What clarity actually gives you

Once you know where you stand, anxiety usually shifts from a vague, constant hum into a specific, addressable problem — which is a different and lighter thing to carry. "I don't know if I'm okay" becomes "here's my situation, and here's what would improve it," which is a much more workable starting point.

Where this article's limits are

If money anxiety feels persistent, overwhelming, or is affecting daily life beyond financial decisions, that's worth a conversation with a GP or counsellor — this kind of ongoing distress deserves proper support, not just a spreadsheet. For the financial side specifically, though, clarity really is the most effective first move available to you today.

The number you're afraid to look at is almost never as bad as the number you've imagined.
