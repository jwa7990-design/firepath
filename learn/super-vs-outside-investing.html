Should I Put Extra Into Super or Invest Outside It?

Part of the FirePath Learning Lab · Educational only, not financial advice · Browse all topics →

This is a genuine trade-off, not a question with one universally correct answer — it depends heavily on your timeline and how much you value accessibility versus tax efficiency.

The case for extra super contributions

Money contributed to super before tax (via salary sacrifice) is generally taxed at a flat 15% inside super, rather than your marginal income tax rate — which can be a meaningful saving for many income levels. Investment earnings inside super are also taxed concessionally compared to earnings outside it.

Common misconception

"Maximising super contributions is always the smartest move." Only if you won't need that money before preservation age. For anyone planning to reduce hours or retire early before 60, locking extra money in super can work directly against that specific goal.

A quick example

Two colleagues on the same salary. One salary-sacrifices heavily into super, saving real tax but locking that money until 60. The other invests the same amount outside super, paying more tax on it but keeping it fully accessible. The first genuinely builds more total wealth by 60 due to the tax saving — but if either of them wants to stop working at 50, only the second one actually can, because their money isn't locked away.

The case for investing outside super

If your goal involves stopping work before your preservation age, money inside super simply isn't available to fund that period, no matter how large the balance. Building savings outside super gives you the flexibility to access funds during an earlier retirement phase, or for any other purpose along the way.

How people often resolve this

A common approach is a deliberate split: some extra contributions into super for the tax benefit and long-term compounding, while continuing to build accessible investments outside it for flexibility and any gap before preservation age. There's no universal ratio — it depends on how early you want genuine access to your money. See How Does Salary Sacrifice Actually Work? for the mechanics of the super side of that split.

The right split isn't about which one is "smarter" — it's about how early you want your money back in your own hands.
