What If I Sold My House — Could I Retire?

Part of the FirePath Learning Lab · Educational only, not financial advice · Browse all topics →

This is exactly the kind of big, specific "what if" that deserves real modelling rather than a gut reaction in either direction.

The basic calculation

Selling a home releases the equity built up in it as cash — minus selling costs and whatever new housing arrangement replaces it (a cheaper property, or renting). Whatever's left over becomes a lump sum that could be invested, potentially moving you meaningfully closer to your freedom number depending on the size of that released equity.

Common misconception

"The sale price is the number that matters." It isn't — what matters is equity released minus new housing costs. A $900,000 sale with a $700,000 replacement purchase releases far less than the headline price suggests.

A quick example

Deb and Rob's home is worth $850,000 with $200,000 remaining on the mortgage — $650,000 in equity. Selling and moving to a $480,000 townhouse, after costs, releases roughly $150,000 to invest. That $150,000, projected forward, shaves a few years off their freedom timeline — meaningful, but far short of the $850,000 headline figure that first caught their attention.

What this doesn't solve on its own

If the plan involves renting afterward rather than buying something smaller, ongoing rent becomes a new line item in your retirement spending — which raises your target freedom number even as the lump sum grows your invested assets. Both sides of that equation need to be modelled together, not separately. See Does Renting Forever Ruin Your Retirement? for how to factor that in properly.

Why this is a genuine "Hear Me Out" scenario

Big, irreversible moves like this deserve proper modelling with your actual numbers — current equity, likely sale costs, replacement housing cost, and how the resulting lump sum changes your freedom timeline — rather than a rough guess based on the headline sale price alone.

The number that matters isn't what your house sells for. It's what's actually left over once the dust settles.
