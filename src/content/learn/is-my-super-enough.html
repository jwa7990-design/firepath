"Super is basically retirement sorted." Or: "Super definitely won't be enough, you need to do more." Both get repeated constantly, and both are guesses dressed up as certainty. For many Australians, super alone isn't quite enough for the retirement lifestyle they picture. Checking your own numbers tends to tell you more than assuming either way.

Super as one of two timelines

A useful way to think about this: most people are building wealth on two separate timelines at once — savings and investments outside super (accessible any time), and super itself (generally out of reach until preservation age, currently 60 for most people). Checking whether "super is enough" really means checking whether the super timeline alone covers your target retirement spending from the age you can get to it.

Common misconception

"Super is designed to be enough on its own." For many people it covers a meaningful share of retirement needs but not all of it — particularly for anyone planning to stop working before preservation age, or aiming for a higher spending lifestyle than the system's default assumptions.

A quick example

Projected forward, David's super reaches roughly $780,000 by 60 — comfortably above the 25x-spending target for his planned $28,000-a-year "super-funded" portion of retirement. But he wants $60,000 a year overall, meaning $32,000 a year still needs to come from savings outside super. Super alone clearly covers part of the picture, but not the whole thing.

How to check

Project your current super balance and contributions forward to your target retirement age, and compare the result to 25 times your planned annual spending from that age onwards. If the projected balance covers it, super alone may be sufficient for that phase of retirement. Some people also build savings outside super, for access before that age and general flexibility. Others lean more on super, because contributions and earnings inside it are generally taxed at up to 15%, which can be less than their marginal rate. If you're planning to stop working before 60, What Happens to Your Money Before You Can Access Super? covers the gap that creates.

Things to weigh up

  • What do you expect to spend each year once you can get to your super?
  • Are you hoping to stop work before 60?
  • How much of your money would you want to be able to reach before then?
  • Could the Age Pension play a part later? It's means-tested on your assets and income, so it may or may not.

Everyone’s situation is different. A licensed financial adviser can help you weigh these up for your circumstances.

Super does a lot of the heavy lifting. Whether it's enough comes down to what you plan to spend each year.
