Enough. One word, endless disagreement. Ask ten people and you'll get ten different feelings, and almost no actual numbers. That's the problem — "enough" sounds philosophical, but it has a concrete, calculable answer once you're willing to define what your life needs to look like.

Why "enough" keeps moving

Left undefined, "enough" tends to keep growing alongside income — a phenomenon often called lifestyle inflation, or the "hedonic treadmill." Each pay rise quietly raises the baseline of what feels necessary, so the target never actually arrives no matter how much is earned.

Common misconception

"Enough" is a feeling I'll recognise when I get there." Without a defined number, most people never recognise it — the target quietly moves with every raise, which is exactly why it needs to be written down as an actual figure, not left as a vibe.

A quick example

When Nadia earned $70,000, she assumed "enough" meant $100,000. She now earns $105,000 — and "enough" quietly became $130,000 in her head, without her ever deciding that on purpose. Only once she sat down and calculated an actual annual spending figure ($68,000, covering everything she genuinely valued) did "enough" stop moving: 25 times that is $1.7 million, a real number instead of a receding horizon.

Turning a vague feeling into an actual figure

This means sitting down and deciding: what does a good year look like, in dollars? Housing, food, travel, hobbies, giving, whatever matters to you specifically — not someone else's version of a good life. Once that figure exists, "enough" stops being philosophical and becomes 25 times that number, the same formula covered in How Much Money Do I Actually Need to Retire in Australia?

The uncomfortable part of this exercise

Doing this honestly sometimes reveals that "enough" is smaller than expected — because a lot of assumed "necessary" spending turns out to be habit rather than genuine value. Other times it reveals the opposite: that a bigger number is genuinely justified because certain things matter deeply.

The biggest financial milestone isn't becoming rich. It's knowing exactly what "enough" means for you.
