Enough. One word, endless disagreement. Ask ten people and you'll get ten different feelings, and almost no real numbers. That's the problem. "Enough" sounds philosophical, but it has a concrete answer you can work out once you decide what your life needs to look like.

Why "enough" keeps moving

Left undefined, "enough" tends to keep growing alongside income — a phenomenon often called lifestyle inflation, or the "hedonic treadmill." Each pay rise quietly raises the baseline of what feels necessary, so the target never arrives no matter how much is earned.

Common misconception

"'Enough' is a feeling I'll recognise when I get there." Without a defined number, most people never recognise it. The target quietly moves with every raise, which is why some people write it down as a real figure.

A quick example

When Nadia earned $70,000, she assumed "enough" meant $100,000. She now earns $105,000 — and "enough" quietly became $130,000 in her head, without her ever deciding that on purpose. Only once she sat down and worked out a real yearly spending figure ($68,000, covering everything she valued) did "enough" stop moving: 25 times that is $1.7 million, a real number instead of a receding horizon.

Turning a vague feeling into a real figure

For a lot of people, it starts with one question: what does a good year look like, in dollars? Housing, food, travel, hobbies, giving, whatever matters to you specifically — not someone else's version of a good life. Once that figure exists, "enough" stops being philosophical and becomes 25 times that number, the same formula covered in How Much Money Do I Actually Need to Retire in Australia?

The uncomfortable part of this exercise

Doing this honestly sometimes reveals that "enough" is smaller than expected — because a lot of assumed "necessary" spending turns out to be habit rather than something you value. Other times it reveals the opposite: that a bigger number is justified because certain things matter deeply.

Knowing exactly what "enough" means for you is a big milestone in its own right.
