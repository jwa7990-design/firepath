The Age Pension eligibility age and your actual retirement date are two entirely separate numbers — yet most people have only ever heard the first one.

Two different questions, often confused

"When am I eligible for the Age Pension" is a government-set figure that applies regardless of your personal finances. "When can I stop working" is a personal calculation based on your savings, spending, and investment returns — and for many people planning ahead, it lands well before pension age; for others, later.

Common misconception

"I can't retire before 67 because that's the pension age." The pension age only determines when the government payment starts — it has no bearing on when your own investments can support you. Plenty of people retire decades before ever seeing a pension.

A quick example

Tom, 34, wants $65,000 a year in retirement — a $1.625 million target. Saving aggressively at a 40% rate, his projection lands at age 52. That's 15 years before pension eligibility, funded entirely by his own investments. His "real" retirement date and the "official" one aren't remotely the same conversation.

The Australian two-timeline reality

Because superannuation is generally locked until your preservation age (60 for most people currently), many Australians effectively have two retirement dates running in parallel: the date your accessible savings and investments outside super can cover your spending, and the date super becomes available to take over. If the first date comes well before 60, that gap needs to be funded from non-super savings — see What Happens to Your Money Before You Can Access Super? for exactly how to plan around it.

Working out your own number

Your real retirement date depends on: your planned annual spending (times 25 for your target), your current savings and investments, your ongoing savings rate, and a reasonable investment return assumption. Run those through a proper compounding calculation and you get an actual year — not "whenever the government says," and not a guess.

You don't retire because you reach a certain birthday. You retire because your money reaches the job you've asked it to do.
