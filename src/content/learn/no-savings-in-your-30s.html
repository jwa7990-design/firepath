Two answers get thrown at this question. "That's completely normal, don't worry about it." Or: "You're already behind and it's going to hurt." Both miss the point. The real answer is a number you can work out, and it's more workable than either extreme suggests.

Why this happens to a lot of people

Study debt, career changes, lower starting salaries, periods of unemployment, health issues, caring responsibilities, or never getting any money guidance early on. All of these delay savings, and all of them are common. Arriving at your 30s with little or nothing saved isn't a character flaw. It usually comes down to circumstances.

Common misconception

"If I haven't started by 30, it's basically too late." It's not close to too late. Someone starting at 32 still has 30+ years of compounding ahead of them. That's a long runway by any measure.

A quick example

Liam is 33 with $0 saved, having only stabilised financially in the past year after some rough years. Starting now with a modest $400 a month at 7% growth, by 63 that alone builds to roughly $470,000 — before counting super or any future increases in his contribution rate. Zero at 33 is just his starting line, with plenty of road ahead.

The maths of starting now

Thirty-five years of compounding (from 30 to 65) at a reasonable long-term return still turns even modest, consistent contributions into a substantial portfolio. The earlier "wasted" years matter less than the years you have left, provided you start. If you're wondering whether even 40 is too late a comparison point, Is It Too Late to Start Investing at 40? covers that specifically.

What helps from here

Two things move the needle more than anything else at this stage: building any savings rate at all (even a modest one, consistently), and automating it so it doesn't rely on willpower each fortnight. The gap closes faster than most people expect once momentum builds.

Zero saved at 30 is just where you're starting from. What you do next matters far more.
