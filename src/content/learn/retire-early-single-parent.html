Picture the FIRE blog post you just read: dual income, no kids, 50% savings rate, retired at 34. Now picture your actual life. If those two things feel like they're describing different planets, that's because they usually are. Most FIRE content simply isn't written for single parents — but the maths still works. It just starts from a different place.

Why the generic FIRE timeline doesn't apply cleanly

Single income, real childcare and living costs, and less flexibility around hours all change the equation compared to a dual-income, no-dependants household saving 50% of their pay. Comparing your timeline to those stories isn't useful — the starting conditions are different.

Common misconception

"FIRE isn't realistic for single parents." Full early retirement on an aggressive timeline may be harder, but partial financial independence — genuinely more choice over hours and roles — is realistic for most single parents willing to run their own numbers rather than borrow someone else's.

A quick example

Kate, a single mother of two on $78,000, assumed FIRE was simply off the table after seeing dual-income households saving 45% of their pay. Running her actual numbers — a sustainable 12% savings rate, factoring in Family Tax Benefit — put her on track for meaningful part-time flexibility by her early 50s. Not the aggressive 40s retirement she'd seen online, but a real, achievable improvement to her situation.

What actually helps model this properly

Running your own numbers — actual income, actual costs, actual savings rate — rather than a borrowed target gives an honest picture specific to your situation. Government supports relevant to single parents (Family Tax Benefit, Parenting Payment where eligible) are also worth factoring into a realistic budget, though eligibility details are worth checking directly via Services Australia since they change. For how kids specifically shift the timeline, How Does Having Kids Change Your Retirement Timeline? goes further.

Even a modest, sustainable savings rate compounds meaningfully over the working years most single parents still have ahead. The timeline may look different to the forums — that doesn't make the progress any less real.
