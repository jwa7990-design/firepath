Salary sacrifice is one of those terms thrown around often but rarely explained clearly. The mechanics are fairly simple once broken down.

What's going on

Salary sacrifice into super means arranging with your employer to redirect part of your pre-tax salary directly into your super fund, instead of receiving it as take-home pay. That redirected amount is taxed at a flat rate inside super (generally 15%) rather than at your marginal income tax rate. For many income levels, that's lower than the rate the money would otherwise be taxed at.

Common misconception

"Salary sacrifice is complicated financial engineering." It's just a redirection. Pre-tax dollars go into super instead of your bank account, taxed at 15% instead of your marginal rate. The concept is simpler than the name makes it sound.

A quick example

On a marginal tax rate of 30% (plus Medicare levy), salary sacrificing $200 a fortnight into super instead of taking it as pay means that money is taxed at 15% instead. That's a meaningful saving on every dollar redirected, compounding inside super over the years until preservation age.

The real trade-off

The money is taxed more favourably, but it also becomes subject to super's preservation rules, so you generally can't get to it until you reach preservation age. This makes salary sacrifice a strong option for money you're comfortable not touching until retirement, and a poor fit for savings you might need access to sooner.

Limits worth being aware of

There's an annual cap on concessional (before-tax) contributions, which covers both your employer's Super Guarantee payments and any salary sacrifice. Going over it can mean extra tax. The exact current-year cap is worth checking directly via the ATO or your super fund, since it can change between financial years. For how this weighs up against investing outside super instead, Should I Put Extra Into Super or Invest Outside It? covers that decision directly.

Used within your cap, with money you won't need before preservation age, salary sacrifice is an effective tool. Just go in knowing the trade-off: that money stays in super until you can get to it.
