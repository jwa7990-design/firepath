A common, quiet belief stops a lot of people from investing: the assumption that you need thousands of dollars before it's "worth it." That assumption is largely outdated.

The myth of needing a large lump sum

Many investment platforms now let you invest fairly small amounts on a regular basis. What matters at the start is a steady habit, more than a big deposit. A smaller amount invested regularly for a long period generally builds more wealth than waiting years to accumulate a "worthy" lump sum.

Common misconception

"I'll start once I have $5,000 saved up." Waiting to accumulate a lump sum often costs more in lost compounding time than it gains in convenience. The years you wait rarely get made up later.

A quick example

Two friends, same age. One waits three years to save a $6,000 lump sum before investing it. The other starts immediately with $40 a week. By year ten, the person who started small and early has generally built a larger balance than the lump-sum waiter — purely because of the extra three years of compounding, even though their total contributions were similar.

Fees are worth checking, not fearing

Some investment products have minimum amounts or fee structures that matter more at very small balances — worth checking before choosing where to start, since a high fixed fee on a tiny balance can eat into returns disproportionately. A few minutes of research usually covers it, and fees alone are rarely a reason to wait years.

The real takeaway

The honest, unglamorous answer is: whatever amount you can commit to consistently, started now, generally beats a larger amount started later. If you're still unsure where to begin in practice, How Do I Start Investing With No Experience? walks through the practical first steps.

The habit matters more than the amount. Even a small start counts.
