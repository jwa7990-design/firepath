Renting long-term into retirement carries real, specific risks. But "ruined" is too strong a word for a plan that's just built differently to the traditional own-your-home model.

The real risk, named honestly

Rent tends to rise over time, and unlike a paid-off mortgage, there's no point where housing costs drop to near-zero in retirement. So a renting-forever retirement plan needs a bigger nest egg to keep covering rent for life, compared to someone who owns their home outright by retirement.

Common misconception

"You can't retire comfortably if you rent forever." You can. Your freedom number just needs to include rent as an ongoing cost, rather than assuming it disappears the way a mortgage eventually does.

A quick example

Two retirees plan to spend $50,000 a year. One owns their home outright, so that $50,000 excludes housing. The other plans to rent, and needs to add $22,000 a year in rent on top — making their real target $72,000, and their freedom number 25x that: $1.8 million versus $1.25 million. Same lifestyle otherwise, very different nest egg needed, purely because of rent.

What needs to be true for this to work

Your retirement number (25x spending) needs to include realistic ongoing rent as part of that annual spending figure, rather than just your living costs without housing.

Why this gets missed

Many retirement calculations implicitly assume home ownership by retirement (rent excluded from spending, mortgage paid off). If renting is your long-term plan, that assumption needs correcting. Otherwise your freedom number will be too low for the real cost of living you're planning for. For the broader cost comparison, Is Renting Cheaper Than Owning Long-Term? covers the full picture.

Things to weigh up

  • What might rent cost by the time you retire, and where would you live?
  • How secure would long-term renting feel to you in your 70s and 80s?
  • Could the Age Pension play a part? It's means-tested on your assets and income.
  • Would you consider buying later, or moving somewhere cheaper in retirement?

Everyone’s situation is different. A licensed financial adviser can help you weigh these up for your circumstances.

Renting forever can work fine in retirement. It just means you need a bigger nest egg, and a clear plan for the rent.
