There's no one right answer here. It mostly comes down to when you want the money, and how much the tax savings matter to you.

The case for extra super contributions

Money contributed to super before tax (via salary sacrifice) is generally taxed at a flat 15% inside super, rather than your marginal income tax rate — which can be a meaningful saving for many income levels. Investment earnings inside super are also taxed at up to 15%, which is usually less than you'd pay on earnings outside it.

Common misconception

"Maximising super contributions is always the smartest move." It depends on when you'll want the money. Super's tax treatment suits money you won't touch before preservation age. For anyone planning to reduce hours or retire before 60, money locked in super can't help with that earlier stretch.

A quick example

Two colleagues on the same salary. One salary-sacrifices heavily into super, saving real tax but locking that money until 60. The other invests the same amount outside super, paying more tax on it but keeping it fully accessible. The first builds more total wealth by 60 thanks to the tax saving. But if either of them wants to stop working at 50, only the second one can, because their money isn't locked away.

The case for investing outside super

If your goal involves stopping work before your preservation age, money inside super isn't available to fund that period, no matter how large the balance. Building savings outside super gives you the flexibility to access funds during an earlier retirement phase, or for any other purpose along the way.

How people approach it

Some people put most of their extra savings into super, because they don't plan to stop work before 60 and like the lower tax. Others keep most of it outside super, because they want the option to stop earlier or use the money for something else along the way. Plenty split it, with some in each. There's no universal ratio. It depends on your tax bracket, how early you might want the money, and how you feel about rules that could change over the years. Things to weigh up

  • Is there a chance you'll want to stop or slow down work before 60?
  • How big is the gap between your marginal tax rate and the 15% inside super?
  • Would you want this money for anything else first, like a home or a career break?
  • How comfortable are you with super rules possibly changing before you can use it?

Everyone’s situation is different. A licensed financial adviser can help you weigh these up for your circumstances.
