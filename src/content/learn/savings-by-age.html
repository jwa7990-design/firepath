Most articles answering this question use American figures, American super-equivalents, and American cost of living — none of which map cleanly onto an Australian context. Here's a more grounded, honest version.

Illustrative ranges, not targets

As a rough, heavily caveated guide, some Australian financial educators suggest aiming for roughly half your annual salary saved by 30, one to two times your salary by 40, and three to four times by 50 (including super). These are illustrative only. Healthy ranges vary enormously by career path, location, whether you own property, and life events like study, caring responsibilities, or career breaks.

Common misconception

"These benchmarks are a pass/fail test." They aren't. A 40-year-old with one times salary saved isn't "failing". They're one of millions of people on different life paths.

A quick example

Amelia, 40, has 0.8x her salary saved outside super — technically below the rough benchmark. But she took three years off for study in her late 20s and only started earning properly at 31. Her trajectory since then has been strong: a rising savings rate and consistent investing. The snapshot number undersells a healthy trend.

Why the variation is so wide in Australia

Superannuation adds a layer these benchmarks often ignore. Someone with a lower "visible" savings balance might have a substantial super balance quietly compounding in the background thanks to years of employer contributions. So their real position is stronger than a bank balance alone would suggest. If you want to check your own super trajectory specifically, How Much Super Will I Have at 60? walks through the projection.

What matters more than the snapshot

A single number at a single age tells you far less than the trend. Someone at 35 with modest savings but a rapidly improving savings rate may well be heading somewhere better long term than someone with a larger balance but a stagnant or declining rate.

These numbers are only a rough guide. The direction you're heading matters more than where you are today.
