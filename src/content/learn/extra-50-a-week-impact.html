Fifty dollars a week can feel too small to matter. Modelled honestly over a real timeline, it adds up to more than most people expect.

What $50 a week adds up to

On its own, $50 a week is roughly $2,600 a year — not enormous. But invested consistently over long periods at a reasonable long-term return, compounding turns that steady contribution into a meaningfully larger sum than the simple total of deposits alone, because each year's growth builds on the growth before it.

Common misconception

"$50 a week is too small to bother with." Over 20+ years of compounding, a consistent $50 a week can add up to a significant sum — often surprising people who assumed only large contributions were worth the effort.

A quick example

An extra $50 a week invested consistently for 25 years at a 7% return would grow to roughly $165,000, from contributions totalling just $65,000. On these assumptions, the other $100,000 or so is compounding, though real returns go up and down and aren't guaranteed.

Why the impact goes beyond the dollar total

On these assumptions, on top of the extra money, it can bring a freedom date forward, because it grows your savings and cuts the time needed to reach your target at the same time. The same logic scales up in What Happens If I Get a Pay Rise?

Why consistency matters

A modest amount saved every week adds up steadily, and it doesn't depend on remembering or finding a good moment. A larger amount saved now and then can add up to more, or less, depending on how often it happens. For many people, consistency ends up doing more of the work than the size of any one contribution.

If $50 a week feels doable and a bigger number doesn't, that's a real place to begin. It still counts.
