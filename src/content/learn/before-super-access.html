This is one of the most underdiscussed issues in Australian early retirement planning — and it deserves a proper answer rather than an assumption that "it'll work itself out."

The gap, explained plainly

If you want to stop working significantly before your super preservation age (currently 60 for most people), there's a real gap between when you stop earning income and when super becomes available to help fund your spending. Everything during that gap needs to come from savings and investments held outside super.

Common misconception

"My super balance is big, so I'm ready to retire early." A large super balance doesn't help at all if it's inaccessible for another 15-20 years. Readiness for early retirement depends on your non-super savings specifically, not your total wealth.

A quick example

Elena wants to stop working at 45. Her super, though healthy, is locked until 60 — a 15-year gap. Her actual retirement readiness depends entirely on whether her non-super investments alone can fund 15 years of spending, independent of how strong her super projection looks for the years after 60.

How to plan around this

Rather than calculating one combined "total wealth" number, a more accurate approach calculates two separate figures: enough outside super to cover spending from your stop-work date until preservation age, and enough (in super, projected forward) to cover spending from preservation age onward. Both need to independently stack up. For the full two-timeline framing, When Can I Retire? covers it directly.

Why this matters more the earlier you plan to stop

The earlier the target retirement age, the longer this pre-super gap becomes, and the more of the overall plan depends on savings outside super specifically — meaning super balance alone becomes a less useful single measure of "am I ready" the earlier someone plans to retire.

Your super might be ready for 60. The real question is whether your savings are ready for whatever comes before it.
