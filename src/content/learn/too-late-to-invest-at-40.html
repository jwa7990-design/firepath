"It's too late, you've missed the window." Or: "It's never too late!" Neither of those is a calculation — they're just two competing vibes. The real answer comes from comparing yourself to FIRE stories about people who started at 22, which is exactly the wrong comparison to make.

What the numbers show

Starting at 40 with a normal retirement horizon still leaves 20-25+ years of compounding ahead — a substantial runway. It's less than starting at 22 would have provided, but it's far from "too late." Consistent investing over two decades, at a reasonable long-term return, still builds meaningful wealth.

Common misconception

"Compounding only really works if you start young." Compounding works on however many years you actually have left, not on a fixed ideal starting age. Twenty years is still twenty years of genuine growth.

A quick example

Fiona started investing at 41 with $0 saved, after years of feeling like she'd "missed the window." Investing $900 a month at a 7% return, by 65 that alone builds to roughly $560,000 — before super, before any pay rises redirected into savings, and before any lump sums along the way. Twenty-four years turned out to be a long runway, not a lost cause.

What changes the calculation at 40

A higher savings rate matters more when there's less time for compounding to do the heavy lifting alone — so income and contribution levels carry a bit more weight than they would for someone starting at 22. This isn't a disadvantage to feel bad about; it's simply a different, adjustable lever. If you're comparing your position generally, Am I Behind Financially for My Age? is worth reading alongside this.

The question isn't whether it's too late — it's what savings rate and timeline make sense from here, calculated properly rather than assumed impossible.
