"It's too late, you've missed the window." Or: "It's never too late!" Neither of those is a calculation. They're just two competing vibes. The real answer comes from your own numbers, not from FIRE stories about people who started at 22.

What the numbers show

Starting at 40 with a normal retirement age in mind still leaves 20-25+ years of compounding ahead. That's a long runway. It's less than starting at 22 would have provided, but it's far from "too late." Consistent investing over two decades, at a reasonable long-term return, can still build meaningful wealth, though returns are never guaranteed.

Common misconception

"Compounding only really works if you start young." Compounding works on however many years you have left, whatever age you start. Twenty years is still twenty years of growth.

A quick example

Fiona started investing at 41 with $0 saved, after years of feeling like she'd "missed the window." Investing $900 a month at a 7% return, by 65 that alone could build to roughly $650,000 — before super, before any pay rises redirected into savings, and before any lump sums along the way. Twenty-four years can be plenty of runway.

What changes the calculation at 40

A higher savings rate matters more when there's less time for compounding to do the heavy lifting alone. So income and contribution levels carry a bit more weight than they would for someone starting at 22. That's nothing to feel bad about. It's just something you can adjust. If you're comparing your position generally, Am I Behind Financially for My Age? covers that side.

Things to weigh up

  • How much could you put away each month without straining the budget?
  • How would you feel about a fall in value with 20-odd years to go?
  • How much of your saving suits super, and how much suits money you can reach before 60?
  • Is there high-interest debt or a missing cash buffer competing for the same money?

A licensed financial adviser can help you weigh these up for your circumstances.

From here, it's about finding a savings rate and timeline that work for you. Your own numbers tell you far more than any rule about age.
