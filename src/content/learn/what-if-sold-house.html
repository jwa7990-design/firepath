This is the kind of big, specific "what if" where the numbers can look quite different from the gut feeling, in either direction.

The basic calculation

Selling a home releases the equity built up in it as cash — minus selling costs and whatever new housing arrangement replaces it (a cheaper property, or renting). Whatever's left over becomes a lump sum that could be invested, potentially moving you meaningfully closer to your freedom number depending on the size of that released equity.

Common misconception

"The sale price is the number that matters." It isn't. What matters is the equity released, minus new housing costs. A $900,000 sale with a $700,000 replacement purchase releases far less than the headline price suggests.

A quick example

Deb and Rob's home is worth $850,000 with $200,000 remaining on the mortgage — $650,000 in equity. Selling and moving to a $480,000 townhouse, after costs, releases roughly $150,000 to invest. That $150,000, projected forward, shaves a few years off their freedom timeline. That's meaningful, but far short of the $850,000 headline figure that first caught their attention.

What this doesn't solve on its own

If the plan involves renting afterwards rather than buying something smaller, ongoing rent becomes a new line item in your retirement spending — which raises your target freedom number even as the lump sum grows your invested assets. Both sides of that equation affect the result. See Does Renting Forever Ruin Your Retirement? for how that plays out.

Exploring it in What if…?

Big, hard-to-reverse moves like this are where people often find it helpful to model their own numbers: current equity, likely sale costs, replacement housing cost, and how the resulting lump sum changes their freedom timeline. A guess from the headline sale price can be a long way off.

Money isn't the only part of it. Some people love the idea of a smaller place, less upkeep or a new area. Others value staying near family, friends and the community they know, or the security of a home that's paid off. Selling and buying also costs a lot in stamp duty and agent fees, and is hard to undo.

Things to weigh up

  • How much equity would actually be left after costs and the next home?
  • Would you buy smaller, or rent, and how would that change your yearly spending?
  • How do you feel about leaving your current area?
  • The Age Pension assets test counts cash and investments but not your home. How might that affect you later?

Everyone’s situation is different. A licensed financial adviser can help you weigh these up for your circumstances.

What counts is what's left once the house is sold and the costs are paid.
