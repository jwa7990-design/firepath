This question gets strong opinions and weak maths in equal measure online. A fair comparison means looking past the monthly repayment-versus-rent headline and into the full picture.

What the simple comparison misses

Comparing a monthly mortgage repayment to monthly rent ignores several real costs and benefits on both sides: the opportunity cost of a deposit that could have been invested instead, ongoing maintenance and rates for owners, equity built as you pay down the loan, and the fact that rent tends to rise over time while a fixed-rate mortgage repayment (on the same loan) doesn't.

Common misconception

"Rent is always dead money, a mortgage always builds wealth." A mortgage builds wealth through equity, yes — but it also comes with interest, maintenance, and rates that are just as "gone" as rent is. The comparison is closer than the slogan suggests.

A quick example

In a market where a $700,000 property rents for $550 a week but would cost $850 a week to service via mortgage plus rates and maintenance, the renter investing that $300 weekly difference (plus the deposit that would have gone towards the purchase) can build a competitive investment balance of their own, depending on how the property and share market perform over the specific years compared.

The fairest comparison

The fairest comparison models total cost of ownership (deposit opportunity cost + mortgage interest + rates + maintenance, minus equity built) against total cost of renting (rent paid over time, with the deposit instead invested) — over the same specific time horizon.

Why the answer varies so much by market

In markets with high property prices relative to rent, the numbers can favour renting-and-investing for a period. In markets with lower purchase prices relative to rent, buying can come out ahead faster. There's no single Australia-wide answer. It depends heavily on the specific city, property type, and time period being compared. For the broader trade-off, Should I Keep Renting or Buy a House? covers the FIRE-specific framing.

Neither renting nor owning always wins. Work it out with your own local numbers before you pick a side.
