A pay rise only moves your retirement date if it changes your savings rate. That sounds obvious, but it's exactly where most of the benefit quietly disappears for a lot of people.

Why a pay rise alone doesn't automatically help

If a pay rise is fully absorbed into higher spending (a nicer car, a bigger place, more frequent takeaway), your savings rate doesn't move, and neither does your retirement timeline, even though your income did. The freedom number calculation cares about the gap between income and spending, not income alone.

Common misconception

"Earning more automatically gets me to retirement sooner." Only the portion you save matters. A $10,000 pay rise fully absorbed into lifestyle upgrades moves your retirement date by exactly zero days.

A quick example

Two colleagues both receive a $8,000 pay rise. One redirects half of it ($4,000 a year) straight into investing, without changing their lifestyle. Projected forward, that alone moves their retirement date roughly a year and a half sooner. The other spends the entire rise on lifestyle upgrades, and their projected date doesn't move at all, despite earning the identical extra income.

How the benefit gets kept

Some people direct some or all of a pay rise straight into savings and investments before it becomes a new fixed cost. Others choose to spend more of it on a better life now. Either way, only the part that's saved moves the projected date.

A habit some people use

One approach some people take: whenever income rises, they lift their savings by at least half of that rise before deciding how to spend the rest. Others prefer more flexibility and decide each time. Either way, it helps to know what each choice does to the numbers. The same underlying maths shows up at smaller scale in How Much Does an Extra $50 a Week Actually Change Things?

Things to weigh up

  • How much of a pay rise would you like to enjoy now?
  • Is there debt, or a cash buffer, you'd want it to go towards first?
  • How much sooner could saving part of it move your projected date?
  • If your spending rises with it, how much would that lift your freedom number?

Everyone’s situation is different. A licensed financial adviser can help you weigh these up for your circumstances.

A pay rise gives you a choice. What you do with it is what counts.
