Buying a house is one of the most common reasons a FIRE timeline runs longer than planned. It's worth understanding why, rather than just feeling like you've gone backwards.

Why it slows you down

A deposit is money that could otherwise have been invested and compounding towards your freedom number. Buying turns it into home equity instead. That's real wealth, but you can't draw an income from it the way you can from investments. Ongoing mortgage repayments, rates, and maintenance also reduce the income available for investing during the loan period.

Common misconception

"Buying a house is a mistake if you're chasing FIRE." It's a trade-off. Housing stability and eventually getting rid of a major expense (a paid-off home) are real benefits, and the cost is a slower path to investments you can live off.

A quick example

Before buying, Nathan was investing $2,200 a month, on track to retire at 49. A $120,000 deposit plus new mortgage repayments dropped that to $900 a month for the following decade. His projected retirement age moved from 49 to 55. That's a six-year delay caused by the house, but now it's a known number he can plan for instead of a vague worry.

How to weigh the trade honestly

The fair comparison is buying a house vs renting and investing the difference. Depending on your market and timeframe, one path may reach financial independence sooner, but the other builds a different kind of security (a paid-off home, no ongoing rent risk in retirement). See Should I Keep Renting or Buy a House? for that full comparison.

The bottom line

A house can delay your FIRE date. But it also changes what retirement looks like once you get there, often reducing the ongoing spending (no rent, no mortgage) that your freedom number needs to cover in the first place.
