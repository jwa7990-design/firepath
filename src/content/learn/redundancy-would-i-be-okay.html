One number answers this entire question: how many months could you cover your costs with zero income? Most people have never calculated it. Working it out can help, because knowing the real number tends to be more reassuring than not knowing.

The concept that answers this: your runway

Your financial "runway" is your accessible savings divided by your monthly essential expenses — the number of months you could cover your costs with no income at all. It's the most useful number for answering "would I be okay."

Common misconception

"I'd be fine, I'll just find another job quickly." Maybe — but "would I be okay" is really asking what happens if that takes longer than expected. Your runway number answers that directly, rather than hoping for the best case.

A quick example

Josh has $18,000 in accessible savings and $4,500 in essential monthly expenses — a runway of exactly four months. Knowing that specific number, rather than a vague sense of "I'd probably manage," let him decide deliberately whether four months felt like enough buffer for his industry, or whether to build towards six.

A common (though personal) target

Some financial educators suggest three to six months of essential expenses held in an accessible emergency fund — though the right amount depends on your job security, industry, and household situation.

What redundancy payouts add to the picture

In many cases, redundancy comes with a statutory or negotiated payout based on length of service. If that applies to you, it can be factored into your runway. Eligibility and amounts vary, so check them against your own award or contract.

If your runway feels short, that's useful to know, and you can work on it. If it's solid, now you know for sure.
