No one can tell you exactly what your super will be at 60, but working out a fair estimate is pretty simple.

The three inputs that drive the projection

Your current super balance, ongoing employer Super Guarantee contributions (currently 12% of your ordinary earnings, paid on top of your salary), and an assumed long-term investment return (commonly modelled around 7% before the concessional tax treatment inside super) — projected forward, compounding, to age 60.

Common misconception

"My super balance today roughly shows what I'll retire with." Not even close. Compounding means the bulk of your eventual balance is typically built in the final 10-15 years before retirement, rather than evenly across your career.

A quick example

At 35, with $85,000 in super and ongoing Super Guarantee contributions on a $90,000 salary, projecting forward at 7% to age 60 lands around $520,000. That's more than six times the current balance, almost all of it from 25 years of compounding on contributions that feel modest in any single year.

Why employer contributions add up more than expected

Employer contributions alone add up more than most people expect over a full working life. The Super Guarantee is one of the more effective forms of forced saving, because it happens automatically, whatever your willpower.

Why this is an estimate, not a guarantee

Investment returns vary year to year and won't follow a smooth average in reality. Some years will be strongly positive, others negative. Career breaks, job changes, and voluntary contributions (or lack of them) also shift the real outcome from any straight-line projection.
