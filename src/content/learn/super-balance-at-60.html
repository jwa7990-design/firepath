No one can tell you exactly what your super will be at 60, but working out a fair estimate is pretty simple.

The three inputs that drive the projection

Your current super balance, ongoing employer Super Guarantee contributions (currently 12% of your ordinary earnings, paid on top of your salary), and an assumed long-term investment return, projected forward, compounding, to age 60. FirePath starts from 7% a year after inflation, then takes off fees and the 15% tax on earnings inside super, which leaves about 5.4% a year. Contributions are also taxed 15% as they go in.

Common misconception

"My super balance today roughly shows what I'll retire with." Not even close. Compounding means the bulk of your eventual balance is typically built in the final 10-15 years before retirement, rather than evenly across your career.

A quick example

At 35, with $85,000 in super and a $90,000 salary, the employer puts in $10,800 a year (12%), or $9,180 after contributions tax. Growing at about 5.4% a year after fees, tax and inflation, that lands around $790,000 at 60 in today's dollars. That's more than nine times the current balance. Left alone, the $85,000 would only grow to about $315,000. The rest comes from 25 years of contributions that feel modest in any single year, plus the growth on them.

Why employer contributions add up more than expected

Employer contributions alone add up more than most people expect over a full working life. The Super Guarantee is one of the more effective forms of forced saving, because it happens automatically, whatever your willpower.

Why this is an estimate, not a guarantee

Investment returns vary year to year and won't follow a smooth average in reality. Some years will be strongly positive, others negative. Career breaks, job changes, and voluntary contributions (or lack of them) also shift the real outcome from any straight-line projection. Most super funds have a projection tool that uses your own balance and contribution history. It's a better starting point than any generic estimate.
