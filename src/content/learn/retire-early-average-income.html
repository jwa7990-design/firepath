FIRE stories often feature high-income tech workers or dual-professional couples — which can make the whole idea feel irrelevant to someone on a typical income. It isn't. The maths still works; it just takes longer and requires a genuinely sustainable savings rate.

Why income level matters less than people assume

The core formula — 25 times your spending, reached via your savings rate — doesn't require a six-figure salary. A modest income with modest, deliberately-chosen spending and a realistic savings rate reaches the same kind of outcome as a higher income with higher spending; it just takes a proportionally longer or shorter time depending on the rate, not the raw dollar figure.

Common misconception

"FIRE requires a six-figure salary." It requires a meaningful gap between income and spending — which is achievable at many income levels, just via a different combination of savings rate and timeline.

A quick example

On a $75,000 salary, Ahmed saves 18% — a strong, sustainable rate for his situation, not an extreme one. That gap alone, invested consistently, projects to financial independence in his late 50s. Not the "retire at 35" stories dominating FIRE forums, but a completely legitimate, achievable outcome on an entirely ordinary income.

What a realistic timeline looks like

On an average income with a genuinely sustainable savings rate (commonly 10-20% for many households), the timeline to financial independence is typically measured in decades rather than years — which is still a completely legitimate, achievable goal, just a longer one than the more extreme FIRE case studies that dominate online discussion.

Average income doesn't mean average outcome is out of reach — it means the plan needs to be realistic about pace rather than borrowing an aggressive timeline built on a very different income level.
