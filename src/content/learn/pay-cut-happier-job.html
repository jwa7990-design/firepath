This is a personal decision with a real financial cost attached. Working out the number can help, so it's neither waved away nor allowed to override everything else.

What changes in the calculation

A lower income, if spending stays roughly the same, reduces your savings rate. That pushes out your projected timeline to financial independence, in rough proportion to how much the income dropped and how much of that was previously going to savings rather than spending.

Common misconception

"A pay cut sets my whole retirement back by years." Often the delay is smaller than the size of the pay cut suggests, because only the part of your income you were saving disappears from the calculation.

A quick example

Considering a $15,000 pay cut to leave a draining corporate role for a smaller, more enjoyable business, Tara modelled it properly rather than guessing. Since $9,000 of that would have gone to savings, her projected retirement date moved back roughly two and a half years. That was a real number, and once it wasn't an abstract fear anymore, she decided she was happy to make that trade.

Why the real cost is often smaller than it feels

People often mentally multiply the pay cut by their entire remaining working life, when the real effect is a shift in the compounding curve. Usually that adds a modest, specific number of years rather than something catastrophic. Seeing the real number instead of an anxious estimate often makes the trade-off feel more manageable.

What this article can't calculate

Career satisfaction, mental health, and time have real value that doesn't show up in a spreadsheet. The financial modelling here is one useful input into the decision, and it's not the only one that matters. If reduced hours rather than a full pay cut is more your situation, Can I Afford to Work Less? covers that specific version.

Things to weigh up

  • How much of the income you'd give up is going to savings now?
  • Would your spending change with the new role?
  • How much later a freedom date would you be comfortable with?
  • What is the current job costing you in health, energy or time?
  • Could fewer hours in your current role be another option?

Everyone’s situation is different. A licensed financial adviser can help you weigh these up for your circumstances.

The cost of happier work is a number you can find out. What it's worth to you is yours to decide.
