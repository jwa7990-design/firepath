What Is Coast FIRE — and Could I Already Be There?

Part of the FirePath Learning Lab · Educational only, not financial advice · Browse all topics →

"You need to keep saving for retirement right up until the day you stop working." That's the assumption almost everyone carries — and for a meaningful number of people, it's already false. Of all the FIRE concepts, Coast FIRE is the one most likely to surprise you.

The actual definition

Coast FIRE is the point where your current invested savings, left completely untouched with zero further contributions, will compound on their own to your full retirement number by a normal retirement age. Once you're there, you don't need to save anything more for retirement specifically — your existing money is already "coasting" to the finish line through investment growth alone.

Common misconception

"Coast FIRE means I've already retired." It doesn't. It means your retirement savings specifically are handled — you likely still need income to cover today's living costs. What changes is that income no longer needs to also fund retirement.

A quick example

Josh, 33, has $180,000 invested and wants to retire at 60 with a $1.6 million freedom number. Projected at 7% with no further contributions, $180,000 reaches $1.6 million right around age 60. Josh has already hit Coast FIRE — a decade earlier than he expected, and years before most people even check.

How to check

Take your current investable balance (outside super, since that's a separate locked timeline), and project it forward at a reasonable long-term return — commonly 7% — to your target retirement age. If that projection alone reaches roughly 25 times your planned retirement spending, you've reached Coast FIRE. If you're specifically wondering whether this means you could reduce hours now, Can I Afford to Work Less? takes that next step.

Why it's often closer than people think

Compounding does most of the work over long timeframes. Someone in their early-to-mid 30s who started investing consistently in their 20s may already be far closer to this threshold than they'd assume, simply because a decade or more of growth is already baked into their existing balance.

You might already be coasting toward freedom without realising it. The only way to know is to actually run the numbers — not guess.
