Am I Behind Financially for My Age?

Part of the FirePath Learning Lab · Educational only, not financial advice · Browse all topics →

Most people hear one of two answers to this question. "You're fine, stop comparing yourself to others." Or: "Yes, and here's a scary statistic to prove it." Neither is quite right. The truth sits in the gap between them — and it's more knowable than either answer suggests.

What "behind" would mean

To be meaningfully behind, you'd need a fixed target everyone is measured against — and there isn't one. Someone who took years off to study, care for family, or recover from illness isn't on the same timeline as someone who started full-time work at 18 with no dependants. Both are valid lives, not a race with the same starting gun.

Common misconception

"Everyone my age has way more saved than me." In reality, most people never disclose their real numbers — what gets compared publicly is usually spending (visible) not savings (invisible). You're rarely comparing like with like.

A quick example

Imagine two 34-year-olds, Ben and Callum. Ben bought a new car last year and posts about his overseas trips. Callum drives a ten-year-old hatchback and rarely posts anything. On paper, Ben looks "ahead." In reality, Callum has $40,000 invested and a 22% savings rate; Ben has $3,000 in savings and a car loan. The visible picture and the real picture were almost opposite.

Illustrative benchmarks, held loosely

If you want a rough sense of where people commonly sit (not a target to hit), many financial educators suggest very approximate net worth ranges of around half your annual income by 30, one to two times by 40, and three to four times by 50 — but these vary enormously by career, location, and life circumstances. For a fuller breakdown by decade, How Much Should I Have Saved by 30, 40, 50? goes deeper.

The thing that actually predicts your future

Your current balance matters far less than your current trajectory. Someone with $10,000 saved and a growing 20% savings rate is in a stronger position long-term than someone with $80,000 saved and a 2% savings rate. Compounding rewards direction and consistency, not where you happened to start.

You're not behind a stranger's highlight reel. You're either moving forward or you're not — and that's the only race that counts.
